# Resume & Cover Letter Standards (2025–2026): research notes for the tailoring engine

Compiled 2026-11-02. Source tiers:
- **[P]** primary: employer, ATS vendor, university career office, academic study
- **[R]** a named recruiter or insider quoted in the press
- **[V]** survey by a career-tool vendor, which has a commercial interest; treat as directional

---

## 1. The first 6–10 seconds

**Evidence**
- Ladders 2018 eye-tracking: recruiters averaged **7.4 s** on the first screen. Job titles drew more fixation than any other element. The best resumes had clear headers, bold titles with bulleted accomplishments, and a summary at the top. The worst had multiple columns, long sentences, clutter and keyword stuffing. Page 2 was read only when page 1 earned it. [P] https://csuglobal.edu/sites/default/files/blog-files/theladders-eyetracking-studyc2.pdf
- Ladders 2012: the scan centres on **name, current title/company, current dates, previous title/company, previous dates, education**. [P] https://www.td.org/content/atd-blog/what-recruiters-look-at-during-the-6-seconds-they-spend-on-your-resume
- A former Meta hiring-committee chair: recruiters spend 5–7 s checking minimum requirements (years, degree, similar titles) and recognisable companies or technologies. [R] https://newsletter.systemdesign.one/p/software-engineer-resume

**Engine rules**
1. **Header:** name, city/state, phone, professional email, LinkedIn (plus GitHub or portfolio when relevant). No street address ("city and state only", Google recruiter Erica Rivera). [R] https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2022/eight-top-resume-tips-from-a-google-recruiter.html
2. **No objective statement** ("That was 1970", Rivera). Use an optional 2–3 line summary tailored to the role. Generic or AI-sounding summaries are a red flag. [R] systemdesign.one
3. **One line per role with title, company and dates**, title in **bold**.
4. **Reverse-chronological** within sections. Order sections by relevance. [P] https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2023/08/HES_resume-and-letter.pdf
5. **Most relevant bullet first** in each role. Bain's Keith Bevans: "make it easy for the reviewer to identify the highlights." [R] https://www.gmac.com/resources/learners/business-careers/career-planning/bcg-bain-recruiters-resume-mistakes
6. **Length:**
   - Campus and early career: **1 page**. Deloitte US [P] https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/careers/join-deloitte/recruiting-tips/developing-great-resume.html ; Google's 2025 student workshop [P] https://www.teach.cs.toronto.edu/ambassadors/blog/2025-events/google_resume_workshop
   - Laszlo Bock: "one page of resume for every 10 years of work experience", two pages at most. [R] https://www.themuse.com/advice/5-huge-resume-mistakes-googles-head-of-hr-sees-all-the-time
   - KPMG UK, experienced hires: "ideally two pages". [P] https://www.kpmgcareers.co.uk/experienced-professional/applying-to-kpmg/application-advice/
   - ResumeGo: two-page resumes were picked 2.3× more often, and the effect grew with seniority. [V] https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/19/resumego-hiring-managers-prefer-candidates-with-two-page-resumes.html
   - **Engine default:** 1 page under ~8 years, 2 pages for senior roles, never 3.
7. **Consistency:** one date format, one font, one bolding scheme (Rivera). No typos (Bock cites a figure that 58% of resumes contain them).
8. **Never fabricate.** Bock: "three credits shy of a college degree is not a degree." Microsoft's candidate code requires honest representation and allows AI use only in ways that reflect true ability. [P] https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips
9. **"NYT test":** no confidential client details. [R] Bock, Muse

## 2. Bullet writing

**Formulas**
- **Google XYZ:** "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]". Bock adds a peer or benchmark comparison where possible. [R] https://www.cnbc.com/2018/08/16/google-recruiters-share-the-3-part-formula-for-stronger-resumes.html ; https://www.weforum.org/stories/2015/04/13-qualities-google-looks-for-in-job-candidates/ . Google still taught XYZ to students in 2025. [P] Toronto workshop
- **STAR / SAR / CAR:** Wharton asks for Situation-Action-Result bullets that pass the **"So what?" test**. [P] https://students.mbacareers.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/2018-Resume-Tips-and-Resume-Book-Process-FINAL.pdf . Amazon recruiters apply the same test. [P] https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-interview-guide
- **Result-first:** "Delivered [business value] using [approach]". Every bullet must survive interview probing. [R] systemdesign.one

**Quantifying without hard numbers**
- **Scope:** budget, team size, stakeholders, markets, accounts. Amazon screens for "scope, complexity, recency, and relevance". [P] https://aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/successful-amazon-job-candidates
- **Scale:** users, transactions, volume, revenue managed.
- **Frequency:** weekly, per quarter, releases.
- **Before/after:** cycle time, error rate, hours removed.
- **Rank or benchmark:** top X%, top rating, versus plan. Deloitte's examples: beat target by 25% YoY, a process that saved 20%. [P]
- **Projected impact:** Wharton allows it when actual results are unknown [P]. The engine must label it "projected".
- Part-time roles: state average hours per week (McKinsey). [P] https://www.mckinsey.com/careers/meet-our-people/careers-blog/amy-r

**Verbs and style**
- Start with an action verb. Write phrases, not sentences. No "I", no articles, no narrative, no lines that start with a date. [P] Harvard HES; Wharton
- Replace weak verbs ("assisted," "helped," "responsible for") and vague words ("multiple," "numerous," "very," "key"). [P] Wharton
- No self-labels like "visionary" or "rockstar", which EY says sound insincere. [P] https://www.ey.com/en_ca/careers/student-programs/recruitment-and-interview-tips
- Back every trait with evidence. Bevans: "If you choose to say you are a 'problem solver', I'd expect to see evidence of that." [R] GMAC
- Vary the verbs and sentence lengths.

**AI and cliché tells to flag and rewrite.** Allow at most one per resume, and only if the candidate insists.
- Verbs: *spearheaded, orchestrated, leveraged, utilized, fostered, empowered, championed, harnessed, delved*
- Adjectives: *transformative, dynamic, vibrant, cutting-edge, multifaceted, seamless, robust, pivotal, intricate*
- Phrases: *synergy, results-oriented professional, strategic visionary, thought leader, game-changer, unwavering commitment, passionate about, at the intersection of, proven track record*
- Vague outcomes: "significant growth," "enhanced efficiency"
- Structural tells: identical Action+Result rhythm in every bullet; a summary or skills block that repeats the job ad's keywords; random bold on numbers; heavy em dashes; a CEO-style summary over junior-level bullets.

Sources for the list:
- Enhancv's roundup of recruiter observations. [V] https://enhancv.com/blog/signs-of-ai-generated-resume/
- Words that spiked in academic writing after ChatGPT: "delves," "showcasing," "underscores" (Kobak et al.). [P] https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v2
- "realm," "intricate," "showcasing," "pivotal" (Stanford study). [P] https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/did-chatgpt-write-that-paper-4-words-make-it-easy-to-tell/473651

Why it matters now: in Greenhouse's Oct-2026 report, 60% of US job seekers have changed or considered changing how they write so they don't sound like AI. Its CPO says employers face "AI-polished resumes that all look the same." [P] https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/6-in-10-job-seekers-are-rethinking-how-they-write-to-avoid-sounding-like-ai-even-as-most-use-it-in-their-search

## 4. Honest positioning

**Hard rule:** never retitle a past role. Never write a headline that claims the target title the candidate has not held. Bock and Microsoft both treat misrepresentation as disqualifying (§1).

**Truthful headline patterns**
- `[Actual function] → targeting [target] | [2–3 proof points]`. Example: "Operations Analyst moving into Product | shipped 3 internal tools to 400 users | SQL"
- `[Domain] + [capability] + [evidence]`
- Harvard specifically recommends a summary for career changers. [P] HES

**Pivots**
- Lead with transferable impact. Add a "Relevant Projects" section and only relevant coursework. [P] HES
- Wharton allows skills-based headings for switchers, except in finance. [P]
- Show visible learning (Microsoft/Gardner). [R]

**Founders**
- There is an "entrepreneurship penalty": former founders were 35% less likely to get an interview (LBS), and 60% of recruiters reacted less favourably (Rutgers). [R] https://fortune.com/2024/05/12/entrepreneur-founder-startup-hiring-interview-bias-recruiter-jobs/
- Counter it: acknowledge it directly, show you can work within structure, signal you intend to stay, and map founder duties to the target role.
- **Engine:**
  - Keep the "Founder" title.
  - Write bullets in the target function's language ("owned roadmap," "ran 40 customer interviews").
  - Add a one-line company descriptor (stage, outcome).
  - Add a one-line reason for the transition when the venture has closed. [R] systemdesign.one
- McKinsey actively values founding experience. [P]

**International → US**
- Add a one-line descriptor for employers US recruiters may not know (size, rank). This is inferred from the scan's reliance on recognisable companies.
- Give USD equivalents and absolute scale.

## 5. ATS reality

**Parsing**
- **Greenhouse:**
  - Fails above **2.5 MB** and on image-only files.
  - Breaks on graphics, **tables, headers and footers, text boxes, multi-column layouts** and letter-spacing tricks.
  - Wants full company names (Inc., LLC) and **spelled-out titles** ("Senior Account Executive," not "Sr. Account Exec").
  - A failed parse means manual entry, not rejection. [P] https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse
- **Workday:**
  - Accepts DOC, DOCX, HTML, PDF, TXT.
  - "Autofill with Resume" fills My Experience, which often needs manual fixes.
  - Recruiters keyword-search the database. [V] https://www.tealhq.com/post/workday-resume
- **Lever / iCIMS / Taleo:** the vendor docs were not retrievable. A university guide says only that they "parse differently". Apply the same conservative rules. [P] https://career.uci.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-Mastering-ATS-Career-Guide-8.5x11.pdf

**Format rules**
- Single column, no tables, icons or images, nothing in the header or footer.
- **Headings:** "Experience," "Education," "Skills" (+ "Projects," "Certifications"). [P] UCI; [V] https://jobscan.co/blog/ats-resume
- **File:** text-based PDF or DOCX. UCI warns about Canva and LaTeX output. [P]
- **Dates:** no source mandates a format. Use one consistent `Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY`, on the title line.

**Keywords**
- Use the posting's **exact terms** ("marketer" ≠ "marketing"), the **acronym plus the full term**, and spelled-out degrees. [P] UCI
- Jobscan: resumes containing the posting's job title got 10.6× more interviews. [V] Put the title in the headline as a *target* (§4), never as a false past title.
- Keywords in context only. Ladders and Amazon both penalise stuffing [P]. Never hidden white text. [R] systemdesign.one

**Myths**
- "75% are auto-rejected by ATS" has no traceable source. In 2025 interviews, 92% of 25 US recruiters said their ATS doesn't auto-reject for format or content, and only 8% use score cutoffs. Real auto-rejection comes from **knockout questions**. [V] Enhancv link
- **Counterpoint:** HBS/Accenture *Hidden Workers* found 88% of employers admit rigid filters (gaps, degree requirements) reject qualified people. [P] https://www.hrdive.com/news/report-applicant-tracking-systems-may-exclude-whole-segments-of-candidates/606614/
- Priorities, in order: (1) meet the knockout and minimum-qualification criteria exactly, (2) parse cleanly, (3) match keywords.
- Microsoft's AI match indicator is "a reference point only." [P]

## 6. Cover letters

**When they matter**
- Optional at many top firms: McKinsey says not required, Deloitte often optional. [P]
- Still widely read: 83% of hiring managers read them, 45% read them before the resume, and 72% expect one even when optional (n=625, 2022). [V] https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics
- Tailored letters got ~53% more callbacks than no letter, and generic letters hurt (7,287 applications). [V] https://resumego.net/research/cover-letters
- **Engine policy:**
  - Generate a letter when it is required, for consulting or Big-4 campus roles, for startups, and for pivots or founders.
  - Skip it for FAANG portals that have no letter field.

**Format**
- ≤1 page, about **250–400 words** (~400 preferred [V]). Concise, no flowery language. [P] HES
- **Structure:**
  1. The role, and why you fit *this* team.
  2. One or two concrete examples tied to the job description. Never re-list the resume.
  3. Close with interest and thanks.
  - Same font as the resume, a named addressee where possible. [P] HES
- Always include why *this company*: EY and Deloitte ask for it explicitly, and startups ask why the mission. [P]

**Making it memorable**
- The opening makes the biggest impression (41% [V]).
- **Hooks that work:**
  - (a) A specific result that maps to the team's known problem.
  - (b) Proof of engagement with the product or mission through something the candidate did, not by claiming "passion."
  - (c) For a pivot, the transition in one sentence followed by evidence.
- Ban "I am writing to express my interest" and the §2 cliché list.

## 7. New grad, MBA, international

**New grad**
- 1 page, education near the top.
- Include internships, research (under Experience), leadership, projects and GitHub.
- GPA only if ≥3.5 (Google workshop) or >3.2 (Deloitte). [P]
- XYZ bullets apply to student work too. Open source "stands out." [P]

**MBA**
- Wharton's one-page template: quantified pre-MBA SAR bullets; the school section shows aptitude and leadership with selectivity context; "Additional Interests" line last. Skills-based format allowed for switchers, except in finance. [P] Wharton PDF
- Consulting recruiters want every claimed trait backed by evidence. [R] GMAC

**International → US**
- No photo, date of birth or age, marital status, race or religion. 1–2 pages. List language proficiency and cross-cultural work. [P] https://cdo.pomona.edu/resources/u-s-resume-tips-for-international-students/ ; Harvard HES
- **Visa:**
  - It need not appear on the resume, but must never be misrepresented.
  - Employers may ask about work authorisation and sponsorship, but not nationality or visa type.
  - Use "petition", not "sponsor", for H-1B. [P] https://careercenter.georgetown.edu/diversity-career-resources/international-students/job-search-in-the-us/sharing-your-immigration-status/
  - **Engine:** leave it off by default. If the candidate opts in, add one factual line such as "Authorized to work in the US (F-1 OPT, STEM extension eligible)". Always answer knockout questions truthfully.
- Spell out degrees and majors in US terms. [P] UCI
