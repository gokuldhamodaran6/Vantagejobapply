Extension-first Vantage

Find the job in Vantage. Get a resume and cover letter written for that job, in that country's format. Open the company's site, where the extension fills the form in your own browser and you press Submit. From then on your inbox keeps everything up to date: applications you send anywhere, interview invites and your calendar. 1 · Honest feedback

Mostly the right call. Three parts need changing before we market them.

Agree

Make the extension the main way to apply

Your numbers back this up: the cloud agent sent 19 of 61 applications and 0 of 17 on Workday. The products that work at scale (Simplify, Jobright's autofill) run in the person's own browser. There it has your internet connection and sign-ins, bot checks rarely appear, you clear any that do, and you press Submit. It's also cheaper: no cloud browsers to pay for, which the free server can't scale anyway. It's also safer legally, because you are applying, with help.

Agree

A different resume for every role

This is where Vantage can beat the free autofill tools. Most of the engine exists already: per-job tailoring, a cover letter in your chosen tone, a match check and four layouts that every ATS can read. What's missing is the country format, the Harvard writing rules applied to every bullet, and an honest check of which skills are matched and which are missing.

Change

Don't promise "100% ATS match"

It would mislead people and hurt them. Applicant tracking systems almost never reject on their own: in a 2025 study of 25 US recruiters, 92% said their system doesn't auto-reject for formatting or missing keywords. The real filters are knockout questions (work authorisation, location, licences) and a human reading fast. A "100%" score can only be reached by stuffing keywords or claiming skills you don't have. Recruiters spot the first, and the second is lying on an application. What we can honestly promise: "Written for this job. Readable by every ATS. Covers 14 of 16 key skills. Here are the 2 you don't have." Gaps are shown, never invented.

Adjust

"Harvard standard" is a writing style, not a format for every role

Harvard's career office guide is excellent for bullets: start with an action verb, quantify results, no "I", no photo, no age, reverse-chronological order. But it is written for students: education first, one page. For experienced people, experience comes first and two pages are normal. And the country matters more than Harvard. The US and Canada use a resume on Letter paper with no photo. The UK uses a "CV" on A4 with a personal statement. Germany and much of the EU often expect a photo and personal details. Vantage should pick the format from the job's country and apply the Harvard rules to the writing everywhere.

Adjust

Autofill alone won't sell; Simplify gives it away free

The extension must be fast and must handle Workday's multi-page form. But autofill on its own is a commodity. Vantage's story is all three in one: the right resume for this job, applying in seconds, and an inbox that keeps your job search organised. Simplify does autofill, Teal does resumes ($29 a month for AI), and Huntr does tracking with Gmail. None of the three does all of it with an interview calendar.

Agree, strongest idea

The inbox runs your tracker and calendar

Vantage already reads your job email: it sorts receipts, rejections and interviews and finds interview times. The next step is turning each email into an action. A "thanks for applying" email creates the application, even if you applied on LinkedIn or Indeed. An invite adds the interview to your calendar with prep notes. "Shortlisted" moves the card forward. Two honest limits: connecting Gmail with one click (Google sign-in) needs Google's yearly security assessment, which takes weeks and costs money, so we start with the app-password connection that works today. And a calendar link that you subscribe to once updates Google Calendar only every few hours, while adding the event directly is instant but needs a Google permission.

Adjust

Streaks: reward good applications, not volume

A streak that counts applications teaches people to spam. Employers notice that, and our own rule stops at 3 applications per company per month. Count tailored applications, follow-ups sent and interview prep done. Show a weekly goal ("5 tailored applications") instead of a daily count.

Your call

What happens to the cloud auto-apply agent

My recommendation: take it off the main menu and out of marketing, keep the code, and leave it as an off-by-default beta that only works on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, where it sent 9 of 16. Fully removing it is also fine. Decision 1 at the bottom of this page.

5 · Can we market it?

Yes, as the one tool that does all three

Simplify
  • Autofill in your browser, free
  • Light tracking
  • Basic per-job tailoring at most
Teal
  • Resume builder and match check
  • Job tracker
  • No form autofill; AI features cost $29 a month
Huntr
  • Kanban tracker
  • Gmail email tracking
  • No autofill
Vantage
  • Resume and letter written for each job, in the country's format
  • Autofill in your browser, including Workday
  • Inbox keeps applications and interviews up to date, wherever you applied
  • 895,000 live jobs, with ghost-job and visa filters
Suggested line: “The right resume for every job, applied in seconds, and an inbox that runs your job search.” Claims we can prove: it's tailored to each job, readable by every ATS, and you stay in control of Submit. Claims we can't: “100% ATS”, “guaranteed interviews”.

Competitor features as of 2026 come from comparison articles, some of them written by competitors. I'll check each one against the products themselves before any of it goes on the website.

7 · Decisions for you

Three choices before I build

  1. The cloud auto-apply agent: (a) off-by-default beta, Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby only (recommended), or (b) remove it from the app.
  2. Calendar: (a) start with a calendar link you subscribe to once, which works with Google, Apple and Outlook and updates every few hours (recommended), then add instant Google Calendar sync later, or (b) build instant Google sync first. That needs Google's permission review, which takes 1 to 3 weeks.
  3. Streak: (a) a weekly goal of tailored applications and follow-ups (recommended), or (b) a daily applications streak.
