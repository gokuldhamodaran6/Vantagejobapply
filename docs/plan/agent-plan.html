Why your agent stops, and how it gets through

I went through every application your agent has started since 26 Oct (92 runs) and every step it logged. The main blockers were not CAPTCHAs. They were our own bugs and Workday's long sign-up and form process. Four bugs are fixed today. Below are the evidence, what each blocker really is, and the plan to raise the share of tried applications that get sent from about a third to most of them.

Chart 3 · Workday, decoded

How far each Workday run got

Every Workday application is the same 9 steps. Each company is its own Workday site (for example accenture.wd103.myworkdayjobs.com), so the agent needs a separate account at every company. The dots show your real runs. Green steps are passed now; amber steps are what's left to build.

What the logs show: PayPal and Cisco got through sign-up, contact details and every work and education entry, then ran out of time exactly at Application Questions. The agent had 9 minutes, and doing Workday's dropdowns and date pickers one AI step at a time takes 15 to 20. Mastercard spent 4 minutes in the nested "How did you hear about us?" list. Workday is now allowed 20 minutes (deployed today). The real fix is Phase 1: a dedicated Workday engine that fills these widgets directly, in seconds.

What this means for Vantage: no product publishes an audited completion rate, and none of the legitimate ones fully automates Workday or bot-checked sites. Vantage's edge is a cloud agent that finishes the long forms on its own (the Workday engine). When a site insists on a human, the job reaches you already filled in, one click from done.

Lines we hold

What we won't build, and why it would hurt you

Some "auto-apply" tools beat bot checks with tricks. Those tricks put the risk on the applicant: every application carries your name and email, so the employer sees a flagged application from you. And a company's Workday site can block your email address for every future job there.
